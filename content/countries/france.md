## Two-Round Presidential Voting

French presidents are chosen by direct universal suffrage under a two-round majority system (*scrutin uninominal majoritaire à deux tours*). To qualify for the ballot, each prospective candidate must collect at least **500 valid sponsor signatures (*parrainages*)** from elected officials across at least 30 departments or overseas territories, verified by the Constitutional Council roughly a month before voting. If no candidate secures more than 50% of valid votes in Round 1, the top two advance to a decisive runoff 14 days later.

## Constituency-Based Legislative Voting

All **577 deputies** of the National Assembly are elected in single-member geographic constituencies for a five-year term, unless the President dissolves parliament earlier under Article 12 of the Constitution. A candidate wins outright in Round 1 only with an absolute majority of valid votes representing at least 25% of registered voters; otherwise, candidates who win at least **12.5% of registered voters** (plus the top two if fewer reach that bar) advance to a Round 2 plurality contest, occasionally creating three-way *triangulaires*.

## How the Senate Is Elected

The **348-seat Senate** serves six-year terms renewed roughly by halves every three years (Series 1 and Series 2) via indirect universal suffrage. Roughly **172,000 *grands électeurs***—primarily municipal councillors and mayors alongside deputies, regional councillors, and departmental councillors—vote in each department, using two-round majority rules in smaller departments (1–2 seats) and proportional list representation in larger departments (3+ seats).
